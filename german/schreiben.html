Goethe A2 Schreiben: master templates, phrases and 67 tasks

Templates, phrases and model answers for both parts of the A2 writing test, plus A1 practice.

Goethe-Zertifikat A267 tasksGerman and English

In the Goethe A2 writing test you do not need to be creative. Every SMS and every e-mail has the same frame: greeting, three short sentences, closing. Learn the frame and about 40 ready-made sentences, then change a few words for each task. This page trains exactly that, with 67 tasks, model answers, English translations, word-by-word meanings and pronunciation.

Schreiben lasts 30 minutes for both texts. It comes straight after Lesen (30 minutes) and Hören (about 30 minutes), so the written exam is about 90 minutes in one block. Sprechen (about 15 minutes, usually in pairs) is separate. Write on the answer sheet (Antwortbogen), clearly and not in pencil. No dictionary and no phone.

How your text is marked

Each part is marked on two criteria, usually by two examiners.

CriterionPoints (A–E)What the examiner checks
Aufgabenerfüllung (task completion)5 – 3.5 – 2 – 0.5 – 0Did you answer all 3 points fully? Is the tone right for the person (du/Sie, greeting, closing)?
Sprache (language)5 – 3.5 – 2 – 0.5 – 0Are words and sentences suitable and a little varied? Do mistakes stop the reader understanding you?

Teil 1 gives up to 10 points and Teil 2 up to 10 points. The 20 points are multiplied by 1.25, so Schreiben is worth 25 of the 100 exam points. To pass you need 60 points in total, at least 45 of 75 in the written exam (Lesen, Hören, Schreiben) and at least 15 of 25 in Sprechen. A strong Schreiben can rescue a weak Lesen or Hören. See the full A2 exam pattern.

Zero-point trap. Far too few words (roughly under 10 in Teil 1 or under 15 in Teil 2) or writing about the wrong topic gives 0 points for that part; the language is then not even marked.

Good news from Goethe’s own exam tips: small mistakes are allowed as long as the reader understands you. Capital letters are compulsory, and in Teil 2 greeting and closing are compulsory. If a point asks for two things (place and time), give both.

How to practise: cover and write

  1. ReadRead the German task. Underline who you write to and the three points.
  2. CoverKeep the model answer hidden (it is blurred until you tap “Show answer”). Write your own answer on paper or in the writing pad, using the master template.
  3. CompareShow the model answer and mark every difference.
  4. Repeat tomorrowWrite the same task again from memory, without looking. Then tap “Mark as written”.

10 rules for full marks

1Answer every point – and every PART of a point (Ort und Uhrzeit, danke und ich komme, Tag und Uhrzeit).
2Right person, right form: friend → du; Herr/Frau/company → Sie. Never mix du and Sie in one text.
3Greeting + closing: required in Teil 2; in Teil 1 optional, but always write them – they show the right tone.
41–2 sentences per point. Use the topic words from the task, but make your own sentences.
5Connect and vary: use denn, weil, aber, dass, dann, deshalb; start some sentences with Leider …, Am Samstag …, Dann …
6Word count: SMS about 25–35, E-Mail about 35–50 (with greeting and name). A few extra words are fine; never under 10 / 15 words.
7Capital letters are compulsory: all nouns, Sie / Ihnen / Ihr, first word of each sentence.
8Simple beats complicated: short correct sentences get more points than long broken ones.
9Questions end with „?“ – when the task says „Fragen Sie“, write a real question.
10Write clearly with a pen on the answer sheet and keep 3–4 minutes for checking.

A1 Schreiben: form and short message

The A1 writing test (Start Deutsch 1) takes 20 minutes and has two parts. In Teil 1 you read a short text and fill in five gaps in a form (5 points). In Teil 2 you write a short message of about 30 words that answers three points (10 points). See the full A1 exam pattern.

Teil 1: fill in the form

Read the text, then write the missing information. Spelling of names and numbers must be correct.

Registering for a German course

Bilal Ahmed kommt aus Pakistan. Er ist am 14. März 2001 in Lahore geboren und wohnt jetzt in Köln, Rheinstraße 12. Er möchte am Abend einen Deutschkurs machen, denn am Tag arbeitet er in einem Restaurant. Er spricht Urdu, Englisch und ein bisschen Deutsch.

English translation

Bilal Ahmed comes from Pakistan. He was born on 14 March 2001 in Lahore and now lives in Cologne, Rheinstraße 12. He wants to take a German course in the evening, because he works in a restaurant during the day. He speaks Urdu, English and a little German.

Booking a hotel room for a family

Familie Hussain (Vater, Mutter und zwei Kinder) möchte vom 3. bis 6. August in Hamburg Urlaub machen. Sie brauchen ein Zimmer für vier Personen. Sie kommen mit dem Zug und möchten mit Kreditkarte bezahlen. Frau Hussain hat die Telefonnummer 0176 4421390.

English translation

The Hussain family (father, mother and two children) wants a holiday in Hamburg from 3 to 6 August. They need a room for four people. They are coming by train and want to pay by credit card. Mrs Hussain's phone number is 0176 4421390.

Teil 2: short messages with model answers

A1M1

Greetings from your holiday

to a friend (du)

Aufgabe

Sie sind im Urlaub. Schreiben Sie Ihrer Freundin Lena eine Karte. Schreiben Sie: Wo sind Sie? Wie ist das Wetter? Wann kommen Sie zurück?

Meaning in English

You are on holiday. Write a card to your friend Lena. Write: Where are you? How is the weather? When are you coming back?

Model answer

Liebe Lena,
ich bin in Murree in Pakistan. Das Hotel ist schön und das Essen ist super. Das Wetter ist kalt, aber sonnig. Am Sonntag komme ich zurück. Dann erzähle ich dir alles!
Viele Grüße
Ali

English translation

Dear Lena, I am in Murree in Pakistan. The hotel is nice and the food is great. The weather is cold but sunny. I am coming back on Sunday. Then I will tell you everything! Best wishes, Ali

Words: about 36. Goethe asks for about 30 words.

You are ill and cannot come to the German course tomorrow. Write to your teacher, Ms Weber. Write: Why are you not coming? Until when are you ill? Ask about the homework.

Dear Ms Weber, unfortunately I cannot come to the course tomorrow. I am ill and have a fever. I am staying at home until Friday. Could you please send me the homework? Best wishes, Ali Khan

to a colleague (Sie)

You are late for work today. Write to your colleague, Ms Schulz. Write: Why are you late? When are you coming? Ask Ms Schulz for help.

Ali Khan

Dear Ms Schulz, unfortunately I am late today. My bus is delayed. I will be in the office at 9:30. Could you please welcome my customers at 9? Thanks! Best wishes, Ali Khan

Last-day cheat sheet

Teil 1: master SMS (du, 20–30 words)

Hallo Anna,
es tut mir leid, aber ich kann heute nicht kommen. Ich muss leider länger arbeiten. Können wir uns am Samstag um 15 Uhr vor dem Kino treffen?
Liebe Grüße
Ali

Teil 2: master e-mail (Sie, 30–40 words)

Sehr geehrter Herr Lehmann,
vielen Dank für Ihre Einladung! Ich komme sehr gern zu Ihrer Feier. Ich bringe meinen Bruder mit. Können Sie mir bitte den Weg zu Ihnen beschreiben? Ich freue mich auf die Feier.
Mit freundlichen Grüßen
Ali Khan

Power phrases

  • Es tut mir leid, aber … / Leider …
  • Vielen Dank für …
  • Ich komme gern. / Ich kann nicht kommen.
  • Können wir uns um … treffen?
  • Kannst du / Können Sie mir bitte …?
  • Ich freue mich auf …

Reasons and questions

  • …, denn ich bin krank. (normal order)
  • …, weil ich arbeiten muss. (verb at the end)
  • Wann? Wo? Wie viel kostet …? Um wie viel Uhr …?
  • Was soll ich mitbringen? Wie komme ich zu Ihnen?

Greeting and closing

  • Friend: Hallo / Liebe … / Lieber … → Liebe Grüße + first name
  • Known person (Sie): Liebe Frau … / Lieber Herr … → Viele Grüße + full name
  • Formal: Sehr geehrte Frau … / Sehr geehrter Herr … → Mit freundlichen Grüßen + full name
  • Unknown: Sehr geehrte Damen und Herren,

Word order in four lines

  • The verb is in position 2: Morgen komme ich.
  • Two verbs: Ich muss länger arbeiten.
  • Split verbs: Ich bringe einen Kuchen mit.
  • weil / dass: …, weil ich krank bin.

Final check: three points answered, du or Sie the same in the whole text, nouns with capital letters, greeting and closing, your name, about 25–35 words (SMS) or 35–50 words (e-mail). Viel Erfolg! Good luck.
