IELTS Academic Writing Task 1 and Task 2

Structures, model answers, prompts and a timed writing pad.

Writing lasts 60 minutes. Spend about 20 minutes on Task 1 and 40 minutes on Task 2, because Task 2 counts twice as much.

How Writing is marked

CriterionWhat the examiner looks for
Task Achievement (Task 1) / Task Response (Task 2)Task 1: a clear overview, key features selected and compared accurately. Task 2: a clear position, all parts of the question answered, ideas developed with examples.
Coherence and CohesionLogical paragraphs, one main idea each, and natural linking (however, as a result, whereas) without overusing it.
Lexical ResourceA range of vocabulary used accurately, including less common words and correct collocations.
Grammatical Range and AccuracyA mix of simple and complex sentences, with mostly error-free sentences at higher bands.

Under 150 words in Task 1 or under 250 words in Task 2 lowers your task score. Memorised answers that do not fit the question score poorly.

Task 1: describing data

  1. Paraphrase the questionOne sentence: what the chart shows, where and when.
  2. Write an overviewTwo main trends or differences, without numbers. This is essential for band 6 and above.
  3. Body paragraph 1The most important features with figures.
  4. Body paragraph 2Other features and comparisons. No opinions and no conclusion needed.
PurposeUseful language
OverviewOverall, … / It is clear that … / The most striking feature is …
Increase / decreaserose steadily, climbed sharply, fell slightly, dropped dramatically, levelled off
Comparisonwhereas, while, compared with, twice as many as, the highest / lowest
Approximationjust under a third, roughly half, nearly 90%

Line graph

The graph below shows the percentage of households with internet access in three countries between 2004 and 2024. Summarise the information by selecting and reporting the main features, and make comparisons where relevant.

Model answer (band 8 style)

The line graph compares the proportion of households with internet access in three countries over a twenty-year period from 2004 to 2024.

Overall, access rose steadily in all three countries, and the gap between them narrowed considerably. Country A had the highest level throughout, while Country C showed the most dramatic growth.

In 2004, 62% of households in Country A were connected, compared with 18% in Country B and only 5% in Country C. Country A continued to grow, but more slowly as it approached saturation, reaching 96% by 2024.

Country B followed a strong upward trend, passing the halfway mark around 2012 and climbing to 88% by the end of the period. Country C started very slowly, with access below one-fifth until 2012, but then accelerated sharply, rising from 30% in 2016 to 71% in 2024.

As a result, the difference between the highest and lowest country fell from 57 percentage points in 2004 to 25 points twenty years later.

Bar chart

The chart below shows the average number of hours per week that people in four age groups spent on three free-time activities in one country. Summarise the information by selecting and reporting the main features, and make comparisons where relevant.

Model answer (band 8 style)

The bar chart illustrates how many hours a week people of different ages spent on social media, sport and reading.

The most striking feature is that time spent on social media fell sharply with age, while reading showed the opposite pattern. Sport remained relatively stable across all groups.

The youngest group, aged 16 to 24, spent 18 hours a week on social media, by far the highest figure in the chart. This dropped to 11 hours for those aged 25 to 39, 6 hours for 40 to 59-year-olds and just 3 hours for people over 60.

Reading followed the reverse trend. It rose gradually from 3 hours among the youngest to 5 hours among people in their forties and fifties, before increasing to 9 hours for the oldest group, who read more than they used social media.

Time spent on sport varied little, ranging from 3 to 6 hours. The youngest group was the most active, with 6 hours, whereas the middle-aged group did the least.

Task 2: the essay

Question typeStructure
Opinion (agree or disagree)Introduction with your position → 2 body paragraphs with reasons and examples → conclusion restating your view.
Discuss both viewsIntroduction → view 1 → view 2 → your opinion (in the introduction and conclusion).
Problem and solution / causes and effectsIntroduction → causes or problems → solutions or effects → conclusion.
Advantages and disadvantagesIntroduction → advantages → disadvantages → conclusion (say which is stronger if asked).

Opinion

Some people believe that university education should be free for all students. Others think students should pay for their own studies. Discuss both views and give your own opinion.

Model answer (band 8 style)

Whether university education should be free is a question that divides many societies. While some argue that the state should pay for every student, others believe that those who benefit from a degree should cover the cost. In my view, higher education should be free or very low-cost, but students should contribute in other ways.

On the one hand, there are strong reasons for charging fees. Graduates usually earn more over their lifetime, so it seems fair that they, rather than all taxpayers, should pay for the advantage they gain. Fees can also encourage students to choose their courses carefully and to complete them on time. In addition, universities that receive fees may have more money to invest in teaching and research.

On the other hand, free education gives talented young people from poor families the same chance as wealthy students. When fees are high, many capable students either avoid university or leave with heavy debts that affect their decisions for years. A country also benefits as a whole from having more doctors, engineers and teachers, so education can be seen as an investment rather than a private purchase.

In my opinion, the second argument is stronger. Access to university should depend on ability and effort, not on family income. However, free tuition does not mean no responsibility: students could be expected to maintain good results, and graduates who earn high salaries could repay part of the cost through the tax system.

In conclusion, although charging fees has some advantages, I believe that free or affordable higher education is fairer and more beneficial for society, provided that students take their studies seriously.

Problem and solution

In many cities, traffic congestion is becoming a serious problem. What are the causes of this problem, and what measures could be taken to reduce it?

Model answer (band 8 style)

Traffic jams have become a daily reality in many cities, wasting time and polluting the air. This essay will examine the main causes of congestion and suggest some practical solutions.

The most obvious cause is the rapid growth in the number of private cars. As incomes rise, more families buy vehicles, and in many places owning a car is seen as a sign of success. At the same time, public transport is often slow, crowded or unreliable, so people feel they have no alternative to driving. Poor urban planning makes the situation worse: when homes, offices and shops are far apart, almost every journey requires a vehicle.

Several measures could ease the problem. Firstly, governments should invest in fast and affordable public transport, such as metro lines and dedicated bus lanes, so that it becomes a genuine alternative to the car. Secondly, cities could introduce congestion charges in central areas, which encourage drivers to travel at different times or use other modes. Thirdly, safe cycle lanes and wider pavements can make short trips by bicycle or on foot attractive. Finally, allowing more employees to work from home on some days would reduce rush-hour traffic without any new infrastructure.

In conclusion, congestion is mainly caused by rising car ownership combined with weak public transport and poor planning. A combination of better transport options, pricing and flexible working could make city roads far less crowded.

Advantages and disadvantages

More and more students choose to study in a foreign country. Do the advantages of this outweigh the disadvantages?

Model answer (band 8 style)

Studying abroad has become increasingly popular among young people. Although it brings certain difficulties, I believe that its benefits are greater than its drawbacks.

There are undeniable disadvantages. Living in another country can be expensive, especially when tuition fees, accommodation and travel are combined. Students may also feel lonely and homesick, and adapting to a new language and culture can affect their academic performance in the first months. In addition, some graduates do not return home, which can mean a loss of talent for their own country.

However, the advantages are significant. Students gain access to universities and research facilities that may not exist in their home country. They also develop independence, because they must manage money, solve problems and make decisions without their families nearby. Furthermore, living in another culture improves language skills and teaches people to work with others from different backgrounds, which employers value highly in a global economy.

Many of the disadvantages can also be reduced. Scholarships and part-time work can lower costs, and universities increasingly offer support services for international students. Those who return home often bring back knowledge and international contacts that benefit their country.

In conclusion, while studying abroad involves financial and emotional challenges, the academic, personal and professional gains outweigh them in most cases.

More Task 2 prompts

  1. Some people think that children should learn a foreign language at primary school. Others believe they should start at secondary school. Discuss both views and give your opinion.
  2. Many people now work from home using technology. What are the advantages and disadvantages of this trend?
  3. Governments should spend more money on public transport than on building new roads. To what extent do you agree or disagree?
  4. In some countries, young people are leaving rural areas to live in cities. Why is this happening, and what problems does it cause?
  5. Some people believe that unpaid community service should be a compulsory part of high school programmes. To what extent do you agree or disagree?
  6. Online shopping is replacing shopping in stores. Is this a positive or negative development?
  7. Many people believe that social media has made people less sociable. To what extent do you agree or disagree?
  8. Some say that the most important goal of school is to prepare students for work. Others think school has other important purposes. Discuss both views and give your opinion.
  9. Climate change is a global problem that should be solved by international organisations, not individual countries. To what extent do you agree or disagree?
  10. In many countries, the number of older people is increasing. What problems does this cause, and what can be done about them?
