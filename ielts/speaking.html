IELTS Speaking practice: Parts 1, 2 and 3

Random questions and cue cards with timers and a recorder.

The Speaking test is a face-to-face conversation with an examiner, recorded for marking. It has three parts and lasts 11 to 14 minutes.

PartTimeWhat happens
14–5 minutesQuestions about familiar topics: home, work or studies, hobbies.
23–4 minutesYou get a cue card, have 1 minute to prepare and speak for up to 2 minutes.
34–5 minutesA discussion of more abstract questions linked to the Part 2 topic.

You are marked on fluency and coherence, lexical resource, grammatical range and accuracy and pronunciation, each counting equally.

Recorder

Recordings stay on your device.

Part 1: questions about you

Answer in two or three sentences: give your answer, a reason and a short example. Avoid one-word answers and avoid memorised speeches.

Part 2: the long turn

01:00

Model answer: Describe a skill you would like to learn.

A skill I would really like to learn is speaking German fluently. I have already started with the basics, but I would like to reach a level where I can have a real conversation without thinking about every word.

I would learn it in two ways. First, I would take a structured course to reach the B1 level, because grammar and vocabulary need a system. Second, I would practise speaking every day, for example by talking to language partners online and recording myself to check my pronunciation.

I think it would take me around two years of regular study to become fluent, although I could probably manage everyday situations much sooner.

The main reason I want to learn it is that I am interested in studying and possibly working in Germany. Even if a programme is taught in English, speaking German would make daily life much easier, from opening a bank account to making friends. Apart from that, I simply enjoy the challenge of learning a language. Every time I understand a sentence that was impossible a few weeks earlier, I feel a real sense of progress.

Part 3: discussion

Part 3 questions appear under each cue card. Give a direct answer, explain why, and consider other views: “Some people argue that …, but in my view …”.

Useful language

PurposePhrases
Gaining timeThat’s an interesting question. / Let me think about that for a moment.
Giving opinionsIn my view … / I’d say that … / I’m convinced that …
Comparing past and presentThese days … whereas in the past … / Compared with my parents’ generation …
SpeculatingIt might be because … / I suppose that … / It’s likely that …
Correcting yourselfSorry, what I mean is … / Let me put that another way.
