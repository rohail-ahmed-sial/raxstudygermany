IELTS Academic preparation

Format, scoring and free original practice for all four sections.

IELTS Academic is the version of IELTS for university study and professional registration. It tests Listening, Reading, Writing and Speaking and reports a band from 1 to 9 for each part and overall. This section gives you original practice in the real formats, a band calculator and a full mock test.

Test format

SectionTimeWhat you do
Listening30 minutesFour parts (two conversations and two monologues), 40 questions. Settings are everyday and academic.
Reading60 minutesThree long academic passages, 40 questions.
Writing60 minutesTask 1: describe a graph, table, chart or process (at least 150 words). Task 2: an essay (at least 250 words).
Speaking11–14 minutesThree parts with an examiner: introduction, a two-minute talk, a discussion. May be on another day, within 7 days of the other sections.

The whole test takes about 2 hours and 45 minutes. You can take it on a computer, or on a computer with Writing on paper, depending on the test centre.

How scoring works

Listening and Reading each have 40 one-mark questions; your raw score is converted to a band. Writing and Speaking are marked by trained examiners against four criteria each. Task 2 counts twice as much as Task 1 in Writing.

Overall band rounding: the four bands are averaged. An average ending in .25 rounds up to the next half band; an average ending in .75 rounds up to the next whole band; anything lower rounds down. Example: 6.25 becomes 6.5, and 6.75 becomes 7.0. Try the band calculator.

BandIn plain words
9Expert: full command of English.
8Very good: occasional small errors.
7Good: handles complex language well, with some mistakes.
6Competent: effective overall despite some errors and misunderstandings.
5Modest: partial command, many mistakes, copes with overall meaning.
4Limited: basic competence in familiar situations only.

Does Germany need IELTS?

Not as a general rule. IELTS matters for some routes and institutions, not for every German visa:

  • English-taught degree programmes usually ask for proof of English, often IELTS or TOEFL. Each university sets its own minimum band, so check the programme page.
  • The Opportunity Card (points route) accepts German at A1 or English at B2 as the basic language requirement, and English at C1 or above gives one extra point. See the Opportunity Card guide.
  • The student visa application must show that you meet the language requirement of your study programme, which may be English or German. See the student visa guide.

An eight-week plan

WeeksFocus
1–2Learn the format. One Reading passage and one Listening part a day. Write one Task 1 report per week.
3–4Work on question types you get wrong (True/False/Not given, matching headings). Two Task 2 essays a week.
5–6Timed practice: full Reading in 60 minutes, full Listening once. Record yourself in Speaking Part 2 twice a week.
7–8One full mock test a week; review every mistake. Rest the day before the test.
